Teams on a shellwing server: shared host collections and SSH certificates from your organization. Plus a host list where you can tell hosts apart at a glance.

## Upgrade notes

- **A click on a host goes to its tab.** Clicking a host in the list, or its tile on the folded rail, now goes to that host's last tab instead of opening another one. If the tab shown is already on that host, nothing changes. **Double-click** to open a new tab.
- **shellwing asks each host which system it runs, to show its logo.** Once you are connected, it runs `cat /etc/os-release` and `uname -sr` once in the background, apart from your shell, at most once a day per host. The answer stays on your computer (`state/systems.json`) and is never synced. Choosing an icon for a host stops this for that host.

## Teams, on a shellwing server

- **Sync with a shellwing server.** *Settings › Sync › Connect* signs you in through the browser. It sits beside a folder, WebDAV, S3 and git. You can run your own server: [hivepaas/shellwing-server](https://github.com/hivepaas/shellwing-server).
- **SSH certificates from your organization.** A group, or a single host, can sign in with short-lived certificates from an organization on the server. Set *Certificates from* in the group or host editor, or `org` in `hosts.toml`.
  - When you connect, shellwing makes a key held only in memory, and the server signs it for the accounts your admins granted you (8 hours by default).
  - The certificate is tried before any other key and is renewed before it runs out.
  - The host's `sshd` must trust the organization's certificate authority.
  - When sign-in fails, the error says why: no certificate, an account you were not granted, or a host that does not trust the authority.
- **Shared host collections.** An organization's collections show up as sections of the host list, and are there offline too.
  - With *Edit* or *Manage* access, you can add, change and delete their hosts. *Saved in* chooses where a new host goes: *My hosts* or a collection.
  - With *Manage*, the palette gives the collection's key to the people waiting for it. Compare the fingerprint with the one on their Profile page.
  - When someone loses access, the collection's key is replaced, and shellwing lists what to change.
  - Passwords stay yours: a collection shares the host, never a password.
- **A collection's settings that act on your computer wait for you.** These are held back until you allow them for each host:
  - a proxy command or a command typed after connecting;
  - port forwards;
  - agent forwarding, or trusted X11;
  - accepting a new host key unasked;
  - jump hosts.

  The pane lists what was held back. **Allow and reconnect** connects again with those settings, and you are asked again if they change.

## The host list and tabs

- **Each host is a tile** beside its name, with its address under it, and its status dot on the tile's corner.
- **Icons:** the tile shows the logo of the system the host runs (Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora, Red Hat, Rocky, Alpine, Arch, macOS and 12 more), or an icon you choose: one of those logos, or one of 16 others (server, database, web, router, bastion…). Choose one with *Icon › Change* in the host editor, or set `icon` in `hosts.toml`.
- **Tabs and the tab overview** (Ctrl+Shift+A) show the same tiles, so you can tell your hosts' tabs apart.
- **The host of the tab shown is marked in the list straight away.**
- **The host and group editors** can be dragged wider, up to 1000 px.

## Fixes and more

- **The terminal engine** moves to Ghostty `vt-1.0.1`, which is newer than 1.4.0 and keeps its security fixes.
- **The window** can no longer be made smaller than 600 × 400.
- **Reconnecting** can no longer be cut short by the previous connection's end, which made a password typed right after it connect a third time.
- **"Syncing…" from Sync now** goes away when the sync ends, also with no tab open.
- **A second config directory** (`$SHELLWING_CONFIG_DIR`) keeps its vault key under its own keychain name, instead of replacing the usual app's key.
- **Paid plans:** their prices say *coming soon* until they are set.

## The first time it opens

- **macOS:** signed and notarized. macOS asks once whether to open an app from the internet and says Apple checked it. Choose **Open**.
- **Windows:** this release is not signed. SmartScreen says *Windows protected your PC*: choose **More info › Run anyway**.
- **Linux:** make the AppImage executable (`chmod +x`) and run it. Where FUSE is missing, run it with `--appimage-extract-and-run`. The `.rpm` is not signed, so `dnf` skips its signature check with a warning, and `zypper` needs `--no-gpg-checks`.
